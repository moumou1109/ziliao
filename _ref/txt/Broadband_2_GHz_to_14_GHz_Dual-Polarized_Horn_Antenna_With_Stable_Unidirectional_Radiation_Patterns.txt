Broadband 2 GHz to 14 GHz Dual-Polarized Horn
Antenna With Stable Unidirectional Radiation
Patterns
Xianbo Cao

, Tao Hong
, Senior Member, IEEE,
and Wen Jiang, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—A
broadband
dual-polarized
quad-ridged
horn
(QRH) antenna covering 2 GHz to 14 GHz is proposed for antenna
measurements and electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) testing.
To achieve dual-port broadband impedance matching, a metallic
ring-shaped matching block and two metallic bridges are incor-
porated into the feeding cavity. Additionally, the ridge proﬁle is
optimized using a fourth-order Bezier curve to ensure smooth
impedance transitions, while the sidewall structure is shortened
to mitigate radiation pattern distortion at high frequencies. As a
result of these enhancements, the fabricated antenna prototype
exhibits a reﬂection coefﬁcient below −10 dB, port isolation ex-
ceeding 30 dB, a gain ranging from 6.3 dBi to 19.3 dBi, and stable,
symmetrical radiation patterns without beam splitting across the
entire operating band. Compared with conventional designs, the
proposed antenna achieves both broader bandwidth and improved
radiation stability, thereby addressing typical pattern distortion in
broadband applications.

I. INTRODUCTION
B
ROADBAND horn antennas are widely employed in a
range of applications, including antenna measureme-nts
(serving as both reference and source antennas), electromagnetic
compatibility and interference (EMC/I) testing, communication
systems, satellite tracking systems, reﬂector feed systems, and
electronic warfare (EW) [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. Their popularity
is largely due to their advantageous characteristics such as high
gain, broad bandwidth, strong power-handling capability, stable
radiation patterns, ease of excitation, and relatively straightfor-
ward construction [6].
The original horn antenna exhibited a bandwidth of 3:1 [7],
which was subsequently extended to 12:1 [8], [9]; however,
signiﬁcant radiation pattern distortion was observed. Recent
studies have focused on extending the bandwidth of horn

previous studies [15], [16], broadband horn antennas are prone
to higher-order mode excitation, which can result in pattern
distortion. Therefore, it remains necessary to develop broadband
horn antennas with stable radiation patterns.
In this letter, a dual-polarized broadband QRH antenna with
stable radiation characteristics is proposed and analyzed. A
ring-shaped matching block is introduced at the base of the
feeding cavity to realize broadband performance. Two metallic
bridges are introduced below the ridges to enable simultane-
ous impedance matching at both ports. Furthermore, the ridge
proﬁles are optimized using Bezier curves to ensure smooth
impedance transitions. The sidewall structure is also carefully
shortened to suppress higher-order mode excitation, thereby
further preventing degradation of the radiation pattern. As a
result of these enhancements in the feeding cavity and ﬂared
section, the proposed horn antenna demonstrates the following
performance characteristics:
1) broadband performance with a 7:1 bandwidth ratio;
2) a stable radiation pattern without splitting, exhibiting im-
proved consistency across the entire operating band;
3) reasonable gain, cross-polarization level, and isolation.
II. ANTENNA STRUCTURE DESIGN
This letter employs a modular design methodology, in which
the antenna is divided into a feeding cavity and a ﬂared section.
Each module is independently designed and analyzed to reduce
computational complexity and to accelerate the design process.

To verify the effect of the metallic bridges on the reﬂection
coefﬁcient of the feeding cavity, simulations were conducted

B. Flared Section
The structure of the horn ﬂare section is shown in Fig. 3.
The key parameters of the ﬂared section include the bottom
aperture diameter (Db), the top aperture diameter (Dt), the side-
wall height (H1), ridge height (H2), and the ridge proﬁle. The
aperture and height of the horn ﬂared section directly determine
the ﬂare angle. A larger ﬂare angle reduces the low-frequency
cutoff, but it also introduces signiﬁcant phase errors across the
aperture, thereby degrading the radiation pattern. In contrast, a
smaller ﬂare angle improves the radiation pattern, but results
in reduced gain and an increased low-frequency cutoff, which
makes it difﬁcult to satisfy the 2 GHz lower operating limit.
Consequently, the design of the horn’s ﬂared section should
be carefully optimized to balance cutoff frequency, radiation
pattern stability, and gain, thereby ensuring optimal antenna
performance.
As shown by the red line in Fig. 3, to enable precise control
of the ridge proﬁle, this work employs Bezier curves to deﬁne
the ridge geometry, replacing the exponential curves adopted
in previous works, which pose inherent challenges in precise
control [12], [17], [18]. The start and end points of a Bezier curve
are ﬁxed at the ﬁrst and last control points, while the intermediate
control points attract the curve toward them, making Bezier
curves well-suited for modeling complex shapes. Furthermore,
increasing the curve order introduces additional control points,
thereby allowing more accurate curve shaping. Fig. 4 illustrates
the ﬁnal ridge proﬁle, which is deﬁned using an optimized

An additional structural enhancement involves the reduction
of the sidewall height. This modiﬁcation was made based on
simulations indicating that the E-plane radiation pattern of the
full-sidewall antenna exhibited signiﬁcant distortion at higher
frequencies as a result of higher-order mode excitation. In
contrast, the shortened sidewalls, which lack metallic barri-
ers, suppressed the generation of higher-order modes, thereby
reducing radiation pattern degradation and asymmetry. Fig. 5
illustrates the simulated E-plane radiation patterns of both full-
and shortened-sidewall antennas at 10 GHz and 11 GHz for
Port-X (Port-Y exhibits similar results and is therefore omitted
for brevity). The full-sidewall antenna exhibited asymmetric
radiation patterns at 10 GHz and a pronounced dip at 11 GHz,
attributable to the excitation of higher-order modes caused by
the blockage imposed by the sidewalls. By removing portions
of the sidewalls, the excitation of these modes was effectively
suppressed, thereby ensuring more symmetric and stable radi-
ation patterns. The optimized dimensions of the ﬂared section
are listed in Table II.
