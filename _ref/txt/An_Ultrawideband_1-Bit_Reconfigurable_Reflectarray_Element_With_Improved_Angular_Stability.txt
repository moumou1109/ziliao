An Ultrawideband 1-Bit Reconﬁgurable Reﬂectarray
Element With Improved Angular Stability
Xianbo Cao
, Member, IEEE, Zhou Zhou
, Kai-Da Xu
, Senior Member, IEEE, Xiaosong Liu
, Member, IEEE,
Tao Hong
, Senior Member, IEEE, and Wen Jiang
, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—In this letter, a 1-bit ultrawideband reconﬁgurable
reﬂectarray (RRA) element with improved angular stability is
proposed. Based on the magneto-electric (ME) dipole, a novel
central-patch probe structure is introduced, wherein two diodes are
connected to opposite sides of the probe to control the two resonant
modes. This conﬁguration yields a fully symmetric RRA element,
which balances and stabilizes the current distributions associated
with both resonant modes, thereby enabling an ultrawideband 1-bit
phase response. Furthermore, rows of metallic vias are incorpo-
rated on both sides of the element to enhance the angular stability
of the 1-bit phase bandwidth. Consequently, the proposed RRA
elementachieves a1-bitrelativephasebandwidthof58%(8.97GHz
to 16.44 GHz) and maintains this bandwidth at 33% for oblique
incidence angles up to 50°. To experimentally validate the design,
a 1 × 2 elements prototype was fabricated and characterized
using the rectangular waveguide method. The measured results
exhibit good agreement with the simulations, thereby verifying the
ultra-wideband performance of the proposed element. System-level
simulations of a 16 × 16 array conﬁrm that the proposed element
signiﬁcantly enhances the gain bandwidth and wide-angle scanning
performances. These characteristics make the proposed RRA ele-
ment a strong candidate for future radar, satellite communication,
and reconﬁgurable intelligent surface applications.

I. INTRODUCTION

ITH the continuous advancement of wireless commu-
nication technologies and the widespread deployment
of radar detection systems, antennas for electromagnetic wave
transmission and reception are facing increasingly stringent
performance requirements, including beam-steering capability,
wide bandwidth, and high gain. In recent years, reconﬁgurable
reﬂectarrays (RRAs) have drawn signiﬁcant attention owing to
their ﬂexible beam control capacity, compact size, lightweight

[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7]. Speciﬁcally, the 1-bit RRA—a
type of reﬂectarray that employs only two discrete phase states
(typically 0° and 180°) to manipulate the reﬂection phase—is
characterized by its structural simplicity, low cost, and ease of
design. Providing considerable ﬂexibility in beamforming, it
demonstrates signiﬁcant promise for next-generation commu-

Broadband capability has emerged as a pivotal trend in mod-
ern wireless communications, as broadband antennas enable
efﬁcient operation over extended frequency ranges, thereby en-
hancing system performance and reliability [12]. The 1-bit phase
relativebandwidthofanRRAelement—deﬁnedasthefractional
bandwidth over which the phase difference is maintained within
a speciﬁc tolerance of 180° (e.g., 180° ± 20°)—serves as a crit-
ical determinant of the RRA’s overall broadband performance
[13]. However, the majority of existing 1-bit RRA elements are
predominantly based on tunable resonant structures [14], which
are inherently narrowband and thus restrict further bandwidth
enhancement. To address this challenge, [15] proposed a simpli-
ﬁed 1-bit broadband RRA element utilizing a single p-i-n diode;
however, it yielded a 1-bit relative bandwidth of merely 12%,
which remains insufﬁcient for many broadband applications. In
[16], a multilayer stacked conﬁguration was employed to extend
the phase bandwidth within the Ku band, albeit at the cost of
a higher proﬁle and greater fabrication complexity. Similarly,
a cost-effective design based on an ME dipole structure was
reported in [17]; nevertheless, its asymmetric geometry induces
a nonuniform surface current distribution, thereby constraining
the achievable relative bandwidth.
In this work, a novel central-patch probe located at the
center of the ME dipole is proposed to achieve a fully sym-
metric scattering structure. Two diodes are integrated between
the probe and the electric dipole patches to control the two
resonant modes. Consequently, an ultrawideband 1-bit phase
response spanning 8.97GHz–16.44 GHz is achieved. In addi-
tion, angular-stabilizing structures are introduced to preserve
the 1-bit bandwidth performance under oblique incidence. To
comprehensively evaluate the proposed design, experimental
validation was conducted using a 1 × 2 prototype characterized
via the rectangular waveguide (RWG) method [18], alongside
system-level validation based on a 16 × 16 array simulation.
The results demonstrate the ultra-wideband characteristics and
angular stability of the element, as well as its contribution
to enhancing array performance. To the best of the authors’
knowledge, this design achieves the widest 1-bit phase band-
width among reported X- and Ku-band 1-bit RRA elements,
demonstrating superior performance even under large angles of
incidence.

II. ULTRAWIDEBAND AND ANGULARLY STABLE RRA
ELEMENT
A. Element Conﬁguration
Fig. 1 depicts the detailed structure of the proposed ultraw-
ideband RRA element. The structure is x-polarized and consists
of two dielectric substrates (F4B, ϵr = 2.2, tanδ = 0.007). A
0.1 mm-thick bonding layer (FR4, ϵr = 4.2, tanδ = 0.02) is
placed between the substrates to facilitate fabrication. The upper
layer of Substrate1 comprises two large metallic patches and a
novelcentral-patchprobe.Thetwolargepatchesserveasanelec-
tric dipole, with two symmetrically placed p-i-n diodes bridging
them. Six metallic vias are embedded in Substrate1, forming a
loop-like structure in conjunction with the metal ground plane
between them. This conﬁguration acts as an equivalent magnetic
dipole. The central-patch probe, along with the vias beneath it,
is used for biasing the diodes. A bias voltage is applied from
the dc bias point located on the bottom layer of Substrate2 to
simultaneously switch the two p-i-n diodes between ON and OFF
states, thereby realizing two reconﬁgurable reﬂection states of
the RRA element. It is worth noting that the central-patch probe
renders the proposed element fully symmetric along both the
x- and y-axes, resulting in a more uniform and stable current
distribution for both resonance states, thereby expanding the
operating bandwidth. To prevent high-frequency signals from
leaking into the dc path, a sector-shaped stub is incorporated into
the bottom biasing circuit to serve as an RF choke. Furthermore,
two rows of metallic vias are placed around the element, adjacent
to the electric dipole patches, to enhance angular stability.
A p-i-n diode, model MACOM MA4AGFCP910, is used to
control the reﬂection states of the proposed RRA element in
this design. For ON or OFF state, the p-i-n diode is modeled as
a series of lumped resistance (R) and inductance (L) or capac-
itance (C) and inductance (L), respectively [8]. More detailed

B. Ultrawideband Characteristic and Operating Mechanism
The reﬂection performances in terms of phase and magnitude
under normal incidence are shown in Fig. 2(a). The phase
difference between the two switching states of the RRA element
remains within 180° ± 20° across the 8.97GHz–16.44 GHz fre-
quency range, achieving a 1-bit phase bandwidth of 58%, which
demonstrates ultrawideband performance. Fig. 2(b) illustrates
the amplitude responses of the RRA element in both switching
states. It is evident that the element exhibits a wide resonant
bandwidthinbothstates,consistentwiththebroadbandradiation
characteristics of ME dipoles [20]. In the ON state, the element
exhibits higher loss, primarily due to the inevitable energy dis-
sipation caused by resistive heating as the current ﬂows through
the p-i-n diodes. Nevertheless, the reﬂection magnitudes in both
states are larger than −2 dB, which is quite acceptable for 1-bit
RRA design.

RRA element, current distribution analyses were performed and
discussed. Fig. 3 shows the current distribution of the RRA
element under x-polarized wave incidence. Solid black lines
indicate the directions of the current vectors. It is observed
that the proposed RRA element operates in a magnetic-dipole
resonance mode when the diodes are reverse-biased (OFF state)
and in an electric-dipole resonance mode when forward-biased
(ON state) [17]. It should be noted that the resonance mode
associated with each reconﬁgurable state remains stable across

C. Improved Angular Stability Characteristics
In practical RRA applications, the oblique incidence of elec-
tromagnetic waves is inevitable. Compared to normal incidence,
the performance of an RRA typically deteriorates under oblique
incidence. This degradation is commonly attributed to multiple-
reﬂection interference and the interactions between adjacent
RRA elements [21]. Given that the proposed element utilizes
a structure comprising two dielectric substrates separated by a
metallic ground plane, this analysis primarily focuses on the
mutual coupling between adjacent elements.
To mitigate this effect, two rows of metallic vias are intro-
ducedalongbothsidesoftheelementintheE-plane.Thespacing
between these vias is signiﬁcantly smaller than the operating
wavelength, allowing them to function as an electric wall. This
conﬁguration introduces an additional boundary condition that
effectively suppresses mutual coupling, thereby minimizing per-
formance degradation under oblique incidence.
Fig. 4 compares the simulated reﬂection characteristics with
and without metallic vias under different incident angles (θi).
In the absence of metallic vias, the reﬂection characteristics
exhibit signiﬁcant variations in both amplitude and phase as
θi increases. Speciﬁcally, when θi exceeds 20°, anomalous
resonances—manifested as signiﬁcant ﬂuctuations in the phase
curve and sharp dips in the amplitude curve—occur around
11 GHz, disrupting the continuity of the phase difference curve
and resulting in a substantial reduction in the 1-bit phase band-
width. This phenomenon is primarily attributed to undesired
mutual coupling between adjacent elements, which severely
degrades the reﬂection performance of the RRA and leads to
limited angular stability. With the introduction of metallic vias,
these anomalous resonances are effectively eliminated, and the
reﬂection phase difference becomes signiﬁcantly more stable.
Notably, the element maintains a remarkable relative bandwidth
of 33% (10.05–14.11 GHz), even under oblique incidence angles
up to 50°. This result demonstrates that the incorporation of
metallic vias signiﬁcantly enhances the performance of the RRA
element under oblique incidence.

IV. CONCLUSION
This letter presents a novel 1-bit ultrawideband RRA element
with enhanced angular stability. The element achieves a 58%
1-bit relative phase bandwidth (8.97 GHz to 16.44 GHz) and
retains a 33% bandwidth under oblique incidence up to 50°. To
validate the design, a 1 × 2-element prototype was fabricated
and characterized using the RWG method. The measured results
exhibit good agreement with simulations, thereby verifying the
excellent 1-bit phase bandwidth and the associated low loss.
Furthermore, system-level performance was evaluated through
numerical simulations of a 16 × 16 array. The results con-
ﬁrm that the proposed element signiﬁcantly enhances the gain
bandwidth and wide-angle scanning performance of the RRA
system. These characteristics make the proposed element a
strong candidate for wideband applications in next-generation
wireless systems.
