A Wideband Low-Cost Reconﬁgurable Reﬂectarray
Antenna With 1-Bit Resolution
Bing Jie Xiang
, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Xin Dai
, Student Member, IEEE,
and Kwai-Man Luk
, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—A wideband 1 bit reﬂectarray antenna (RA) with
1
two-dimensional (2-D) electronically beam-scanning capability is
2
presented in this article. Based on the basic structure of the
3
magnetoelectric (ME) dipole antenna, a novel reconﬁgurable
4
RA (RRA) element is proposed for wideband operation by com-
5
bining electric dipole resonance and magnetic dipole resonance.
6
Each element is integrated with only one p-i-n diode to change the
7
types of resonances, leading to a reconﬁgurable reﬂecting phase
8
with a 1 bit resolution. As a demonstration, a wideband electron-
9
ically RRA with 16 × 16 elements is developed, fabricated, and
10
measured. In the proposed RRA, each element is connected with
11
a separate bias line to independently control the working state
12
and reﬂecting phase. Measured results show that the proposed
13
RRA can achieve a 3 dB gain bandwidth of 38.4% with a peak
14
aperture efﬁciency of 24% at 12 GHz. Besides, the proposed
15
antenna can support the wide scanning angles ranging from −60◦
16
to +60◦in the E-plane and 0◦to +60◦in the H-plane within the
17
whole operating bandwidth. The proposed simple design could
18
be a promising candidate for intelligent reﬂective surface (IRS)
19
applications.
20
Index Terms—Beam scanning, low cost, magnetoelectric (ME)
21
dipole antenna, reconﬁgurable, reﬂectarray, wideband.

II. DESIGN OF THE WIDEBAND RRA ELEMENT
117
A. Structure of the 1 bit RRA Element
118
The ME dipole antenna has attracted much research interest
119
since it was proposed [27] due to its simple structure and good
120
radiation performance, such as wideband operating bandwidth,
121
stable high gain, and symmetrical radiation patterns. The basic
122
structure of substrate integrated ME dipole is shown in Fig. 1
123
[28]–[30]. Two pairs of patches are placed horizontally to work
124
as the electric (E) dipole, while the adjacent shorting vias
125
and the ground plane work together as a shorted patch and
126
radiate as a magnetic (M) dipole, which requires the height
127
of the substrate to be about one quarter wavelength [28].
128
An L-shaped feeding probe is used to excite the E dipole
129
and M dipole simultaneously. Therefore, the wide operating
130
bandwidth can be easily achieved by combining the two
131
resonances.
132
Based on the basic ME dipole structure, a wideband RRA
133
element is proposed and its conﬁguration is shown in Fig. 2.
134
It mainly consists of two parts: the ME dipole part and the bias

160

161

162

163

164

165

170

171

172
are bonded by another bonding ﬁlms.

B. Working Principle of the 1 bit RRA Element
174
When different voltages are employed on the dc bias line,
175
the p-i-n diode can be switched ON or OFF. As a result, the
176
left side of Strip II will connect with the shorting point if
177
the p-i-n diode is switched ON, while it will remain an open
178
point if the p-i-n diode is switched OFF. Therefore, different
179
reﬂecting phase responses can be obtained for these two states.
180
To reveal the operating principle of the proposed wideband
181
RRA element, numerical studies are carried out using the
182
full-wave solver ANSYS HFSS. In the simulation, periodic
183
boundary and Floquet ports are assigned to the RRA element.
184
Fig. 3 shows the current distribution on the patches and
185
ground plane when the element is illuminated by normal
186
x-polarized waves. State I denotes the circumstance when
187
Strip II is biased with positive voltages and the p-i-n diode
188
is switched OFF. State II is the circumstance when Strip II is
189
biased with a negative voltage and the p-i-n diode is switched
190
ON. As can be observed from Fig. 3, when the element
191
works under State I, the currents on the patches and ground
192
plane are in the same direction at 10 GHz. Therefore, the
193
whole structure works as an electric dipole resonance and
194
acts as an electric wall with a 180◦reﬂecting phase, while
195
at 14 GHz, the currents on the patches and ground plane are in
196
opposite directions. This means that the element performs like
197
a magnetic dipole resonance and act as a magnetic wall with a
198
0◦reﬂecting phase [31]–[32]. On the contrary, when the p-i-n
199
diode is switched ON and the element works under State II, the
200
currents on the patches and ground are in opposite directions
201
at 10 GHz, introducing a magnetic dipole resonance and a
202
0◦reﬂection phase. Meanwhile, the currents are in the same
203
direction at 14 GHz, resulting in an electric dipole resonance
204
with a 180◦reﬂection phase. The working resonant modes
205
and the corresponding reﬂecting phases of the proposed RRA
206
element are summarized in Table I.
207
According to the analysis above, the proposed RRA can
208
support an electric dipole resonance and a magnetic dipole
209
resonance simultaneously to broaden the working bandwidth.

334
lines. In each test round, the calculated quantized phase
335
compensation scheme is loaded into the FPGA and translated
336
to a voltage map with positive (+1.3 V) and negative voltages
337
(−1.3 V) to control the reﬂecting phase of each RRA element.

433
A wideband 1-bit RRA with 2-D dynamic beam-scanning
434

436

437

438

439

440

441

442
fabricated by the PCB technology and SMT. In the array
443

444

445

446
aperture efﬁciency of 24% and a 3 dB gain bandwidth of
447
38.4%, within which wide-angle beam-scanning capability can
448
be obtained. Since the proposed RRA can achieve a wide
449

450
wide-angle beam-scanning capability, it is a promising candi-
451
date to construct a low-cost, large-scale RRA for intelligent
452
