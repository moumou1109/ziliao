A Wideband 1-Bit Reconﬁgurable Reﬂectarray
Antenna at Ku-Band
Shi-Guo Zhou
, Gang Zhao
, He Xu
, Chuan-Wei Luo
, Ji-Qiu Sun
, Guan-Tao Chen
,

Abstract—In this letter, a wideband high-efﬁciency 1-bit 16 ×
16 reconﬁgurable reﬂectarray antenna (RRA) is proposed. First, a
wideband and low loss 1-bit RRA element is designed. It consists of
double-layer patches printed on two dielectric substrates, respec-
tively, forming a multiresonant behavior to expand the bandwidth.
Due to the relatively thin thickness of the double-layer dielectric
substrate (0.05 λ0), the element has low reﬂection loss. Therefore,
the high efﬁciency and wide bandwidth performances can be ob-
tained simultaneously. The phase difference between the two states
of the RRA element is stable within 180 ± 20° in the frequency
range of 12.9–16.5 GHz. Based on the broadband RRA element, a
16 × 16 RRA was fabricated and measured. Tested results show
that the proposed RRA achieves 22.5% of 1 dB gain bandwidth and
25% of maximum aperture efﬁciency.
Index Terms—Beam scanning, high efﬁciency, p-i-n diode,
reconﬁgurable reﬂectarray, wideband.
I. INTRODUCTION
I
N THE ﬁelds of satellite communications, such as space
explorations and mobile communication, in order to adapt
to the long-distance communication and relatively complicated
space environment, it is essential to design high-gain antennas.
As a new generation of high-gain antenna, the reconﬁgurable
reﬂectarray antenna (RRA) [1] combines the beam-scanning
characteristic of phased array antenna based on the traditional
reﬂectarray antenna [2], [3]. Besides, it also has a lot of merits
of low proﬁle, light weight, low cost, and easy fabrication. The
beam agile capability of RRA can meet the requirements of
automatic scanning in satellite communications. As a high-gain
directional antenna, the RRA has great potential in the applica-
tions of radar and communication systems.
RRA has many advantages, and it has been well veriﬁed that
the reﬂectarray antenna can realize the reconﬁgurable function
through lumped elements [4]–[9]. However, they all have the
disadvantage of narrow bandwidth [10]. In order to increase the
information transmission rate of satellite communications, it is
necessary to enhance the operating bandwidth of the antenna.
For small and medium sized RRAs, the bandwidth of the reﬂec-
tarray element is the main factor limiting the gain bandwidth

of RRA. In terms of the element structure, the common ways
to increase bandwidth include a single-layer multiresonance
structure [11] and a multilayer stacked structure [12]. However,
for the RRA, the single-layer multiresonance structure is not
suitable for the connection between the phase-shifting structure
and lumped components, which increases the difﬁculty of design
and fabrication. Although the bandwidth can be broadened by
increasing the thickness of the dielectric substrate, the thick
dielectric will strongly excite the surface waves and some high-
order modes, which will increase the element loss and reduce
the radiation efﬁciency. For satellite digital broadcasting, the
Ku-band is more conducive to the transmission of digital signals
comparedwiththeC-bandduetoitsbetterstability.However,the
higher frequencies of Ku-band bring in more losses than C-band,
and the loss of lumped element is increased at the same time. In
addition, the errors caused by phase quantization will further
reduce the aperture efﬁciency of the RRA. Therefore, it has
important signiﬁcance to investigate broadband, high-efﬁciency
Ku-band RRA.
Up to now, there have been relatively few studies on Ku-band
broadband high-efﬁciency RRA. Two resonant structures are
employed to broaden the bandwidth of the varactor diode-tuned
element in [13]. By using a square slotted patch loaded with
a p-i-n diode and optimizing the phase-shift distribution at the
center frequency, the RRA achieves wideband characteristics
[14]. Although the gain bandwidth of these structures in [13] and
[14]hasbeenimprovedtosomeextent,theirapertureefﬁciencies
are merely 7.5% and 15.2%, respectively. In [15], the aperture
efﬁciency of the antenna is improved by optimizing the feed
position and the phase distribution. However, the gain bandwidth
of the antenna is still limited. In [16], the aperture efﬁciency
has been improved to 25% by placing p-i-n diode on the back
of the RRA, whereas the 1 dB gain bandwidth of the RRA is
only 3%.
In this letter, a multilayer stacked structure is used to expand
bandwidth. Meanwhile, the p-i-n diode is placed below the
ground plane [14], which not only facilitates the connection
between the two-layer patches and the p-i-n diode but also
improves the aperture efﬁciency of the RRA. In addition, two
thin dielectric substrates are used to reduce the dielectric loss
without affecting the working bandwidth. Based on the RRA
element, a 1-bit RRA operating in Ku-band is proposed. Mea-
sured results show that the 1 dB gain bandwidth of the RRA is
22.5%, and the peak aperture efﬁciency is 25%. The wide-angle
beam-scanning performance reaches ±60°. It can be concluded
from the measured results that the proposed method effectively
improves the gain bandwidth and aperture efﬁciency for the
RRA.

II. WIDEBAND 1-BIT RRA ELEMENT DESIGN
In this letter, the multilayer stacked structure is adopted to
design the RRA element. Two layers of patches form double
resonance behavior, which obtains a smooth and good linear
reﬂection phase curve. The element structure is shown in Fig. 1,
which includes three stacked dielectric substrates and four metal
layers. Arlon AD255 (εr = 2.55 and tanδ = 0.0014) is used
for all three dielectric layers. A rectangular patch and a slotted
square patch are printed on the top surface of the upper and
middle dielectric layers, respectively. The ground plane is placed
between the middle substrate and the bottom substrate, which
is conducive to grounding the dc circuits. The lower surface of
the bottom dielectric substrate contains the dc bias line and two
microstrip lines for connecting p-i-n diode. The two radiating
patches are connected through the metalized via holes, one
microstrip line is connected to the end of the p-i-n diode and
the other one is connected to the other end of the p-i-n diode,
which is ﬁnally connected to the ground plane. The welding
position of the p-i-n diode is shown in Fig. 1(b). The resonant
characteristics of the element change correspondingly when the
p-i-n diode is ON or OFF under the control of the dc bias voltage,
which eventually guarantees the proposed element has a phase
difference of 180° between the two states.
The MACOM MADP-000907-14020W p-i-n diode is chosen
in this design, which can operate in the desired frequency with
low insertion loss. In the element simulation, the p-i-n diode is
modeled as the RLC boundary condition in the High Frequency
Structure Simulator (HFSS) software. For the ON state, the p-i-n
diode is modeled as a lumped resistor with a typical value of
5.2 Ω, while the p-i-n diode is modeled as a lumped capacitance
with a typical value of 25 fF on the OFF state. The bias point
should be designed at the equivalent zero-electric ﬁeld position
of the element to reduce the inﬂuence of the dc signal on the
reﬂection ﬁeld. To isolate the radio frequency signal from the dc
signal, an open-ended radial stub and a quarter-wavelength bias
line with high resistance are connected to the dc line.
The proposed RRA element is simulated by ANSYS HFSS
software with periodic boundary conditions. Some key geomet-
ric parameters are: P = 9.5 mm, t1 = t2 = 1.016 mm, t3 =
0.508 mm, L = W = 4.5 mm, and W1 = 1.26 mm. Under
the condition of vertical incidence, the reﬂection phase and
amplitude results of the RRA element are simulated in the
ON and OFF states, as shown in Fig. 2(a). It can be obtained
from the ﬁgure that a phase difference of 180° ± 20° is achieved

III. 16 × 16 RRA PROTOTYPE DESIGN AND MEASUREMENT
A. 16 × 16 RRA Design
Based on the proposed broadband 1-bit RRA element, a
16 × 16 RRA is designed and fabricated, as shown in Figs. 3
and 4. The RRA array is arranged in a circular pattern, which
consists of 208 reﬂectarray elements with an effective aperture
diameter of 152 mm, corresponding to 7.6 λ0 at 15 GHz. A
total of 16 sockets are arranged on both sides of the array to
connect to the main control board. Every socket contains 16 dc
input channels corresponding to the 16 elements in two adjacent
columns on one side of the RRA array. The size of the printed
circuit board is 271 × 192 mm2. Each element of the reﬂectarray
contains one p-i-n diode, and the total of 208 p-i-n diodes is
soldered on the RRA. Since the RRA is y-polarization, in order
to reduce the inﬂuence of the dc biasing lines on the radiation
performance of the antenna, the dc biasing lines are placed in
the x-axis direction.

C. Feed
In this letter, a standard pyramid horn at the Ku-band is chosen
as feed, which can emit the y-polarized spherical wave. Its
equivalent phase center is 14 mm behind the aperture plane of
the horn, and the 10 dB beamwidth is 52°. Therefore, the focal
diameter ratio is 1.03, and the distance from the equivalent phase
center of the horn to the RRA is 156.6 mm. In order to reduce
the blocking effect, the offset feeding method is adopted. The
horn is located in the xoz plane, and the offset angle is 26°.
D. Optimization of Compensation Phase Distribution
For the reﬂectarray, the spherical wave emitted by the feed
is converted to the plane wave in the (θ0, ϕ0) direction, and the
phase shift of the mnth element is calculated as follows:
ϕrmn = k [dmn −sin θ0 (xmn cos ϕ0 + ymn sin ϕ0)] + Δϕ
(1)
where Δϕ is a constant reference phase [17], k represents the
propagation constant, and dmn is the distance between the mnth
element (xmn, ymn, zmn) and the feed phase center (xf, yf, zf).
According to (1), the continuous phase-shift compensation
of the reﬂectarray is within the range of 0°–360°. Since there
are only two states for each element, the 1-bit discrete phases
are required. In this design, the compensation phases of the two
states at 15 GHz are designed as 90° and 270°, and the standard
180° phase shift is selected as the quantization standard. The
quantization method is shown as follows:

IV. CONCLUSION
In this letter, a wideband high-efﬁciency 1-bit RRA with
16 × 16 elements operating in Ku-band is proposed. In this
design, the RRA element expands the bandwidth by stacking
two layers of radiating patches to form two resonant frequencies
close to each other. Meanwhile, the reconﬁgurable element in
this design does not increase the reﬂection loss by using the
stacking structure, which is conducive to improving the aperture
efﬁciency of RRA. The measured results show that the proposed
RRA has a 1 dB gain bandwidth of 22.5% and an aperture
efﬁciency of 25%.
